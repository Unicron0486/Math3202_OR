	\subsection*{Sets}
	
	\begin{tabular}{rl}
		$S$ & Skills\\
		$J$ & Tasks\\
		$I$ & Technician\\
		$D$ & Days
	\end{tabular}

	\subsection*{Data}
	
	\begin{tabular}{rl}
		$e_{is}$ & score of technician $i\in I$ for skill $s\in S$\\
		$k_j$ & skill required for job $j\in J$\\
		$t_j$ & time required to complete task $j\in J$\\
		$p_j$ & number of technicians required to complete task $j\in J$\\
		$a_j$ & title description of task $j\in J$\\
		$M$ & maximum time available for each technician: 36 hours a week\\
		$Md$ & maximum time available for each technician on day $d\in D$: 10 hours a day\\
		$b$ & bonus score after training: 7


	\end{tabular}

	\subsection*{Variables}
	
	\begin{tabular}{rl}
		$X_{ij}$ & Binary variable of technician $i\in I$ allocated to task $j\in J$\\
		$W_{id}$ & Binary variable of technician $i\in I$ working on day $d\in D$\\
		$Y_{is}$ & Binary variable representing if technician $i\in I$ receives training for skill $s\in S$\\
		$T_i$ & Time allocated to technician $i\in I$ on day $d\in D$\\
		$L_j$ & Total skill allocated to task $j\in J$\\
		$H_{id}$ & Hours worked by a technician $i\in I$ on day $d\in D$\\
	\end{tabular}

	\subsection*{Objective}
	Optimise the skill scores to complete all tasks
	\begin{equation*}
		\max \sum_{j\in J} L_j
	\end{equation*}
	
	\subsection*{Constraints}
	
	\begin{align}
		X_{ij} \in {0,1} & \quad \forall i\in I, j\in J \label{C1}\\
		Y_{is} \in {0,1} & \quad \forall i\in I, s\in S \label{C15}\\
		T_{id} = \sum_{j\in J} t_j . x_{ij} & \quad \forall i\in I \label{C2}\\
		T_{id} \leq M & \quad \forall i\in I \label{C3}\\
		L_j = \sum_{i\in I} (e_{ik} . x_{ij} + Y_{ik} . b) & \quad \forall j\in J k\in k_j \label{C4}\\
		\sum_{i\in I} x_{ij} = p_j & \quad \forall j\in J \label{C5}\\\
		X_{3j} \neq X_{8j} & \quad \forall j\in J \label{C6}\\
		X_{6j} \neq X_{7j} & \quad \forall j\in J \label{C7}\\
		X_{3j} \neq X_{12j} & \quad \forall j\in J \label{C8}\\
		X_{10j} \neq X_{8j} & \quad \forall j\in J \label{C9}\\
		H_{id} <= Md . W_{id} & \quad \forall i\in I, d\in D \label{C10}\\
		\sum_{d\in D} W_{id} \leq 5 & \quad \forall i\in I \label{C11}\\
		X_{ij} + X_{in} \leq 1 & \quad \forall i\in I, j\in J, n\in \{j \mid a_j = \text{Nightly telescope operation}\} \label{C12}\\
		\sum_{i\in I, s\in S} Y_{is} = 5 & \label{C13}\\
		\sum_{s\in S} Y_{is} \leq 1 & \quad \forall i\in I \label{C14}
	\end{align}

	The first two constraints (\ref{C1}, \ref{C15}) specify the binary variables used in the model where (\ref{C15})
	is used for applying the additional training scores. Constraint (\ref{C2}) refers to the total time worked for worker $i$
	Constraint (\ref{C3}) applies the limit of work hours for each worker. The constraint applied to $L_j$ specifies how the
	total skill for each job. As seen in (\ref{C4}) it is the efficiency of worker $i$ in the skill required for job $j$
	with the addition of a skill bonus if the worker $i$ had training.
	The next constraint (\ref{C5}) ensures there is enough technicians to do job $j$.
	From (\ref{C6}) to (\ref{C9}) ensure that certain collegues do not work together.
	Constraint (\ref{C10}) connects the binary variable $W$ and hours worked on day $H$ such that if $W_{id} = 0$ then $H_{id} = 0$
	and if $W_{id} = 1$ then $H_{id}$ can be up to the maximum hours a day $Md$.
	The next constraint (\ref{C11}) ensures workers only work for a maximum of 5 days a week.
	Constraint (\ref{C12}) causes any worker who is rostered on the night shift to have that as thier only job for that day.
	Finally (\ref{C13}) and (\ref{C14}) refer to the the bonus efficiencies given to those who have training, the first constraint 
	limits the number of workers who have training to 5, and the second states that each of the 5 workers chosen must be unique.

\subsection*{Ancestral skies staffing}
In an attempt to keep this report consice, weekly scores are used to indicate the rostering.
In the appendix tables showing the most effective rostering for each day produced by the final model are shown. 

\subsection*{Communication 6}
Given the set of tasks needing to be completed for the week and the time it takes to complete them a 
total skill score acheived by the end of the week was found.
With the application of the constraint that technichans could only work up to 36 hours a week and each task
specifying the required amount of technichans to comlete the job, the score was 2676.9.

\subsection*{Communication 7}
To follow the kinship avoidance practices, additional constraints were put in such that the
technician IDs given in the table below did not work together on a job.
This reduced the total skill score to 2615.4, a decrease of 61.5.

\subsection*{Communication 8}
Shifting into the framework from weekly tasks to daily tasks with the addition of a maximum work load of 10 hours a day 
for each technician, the new score for the week was 2530.4.
There was once again a decrease as previously some technicians with higher skills worked more than 10 hours
a day, but this constraint needs to redistribute the time to each technician such that some jobs may no longer 
get the optimal technician for the job.
To further examine the effect of adding time retrictions to daily work, I obsverved the total skill score that could be 
acheived if you extend the hours up to 11 or down to 9 hours. The scores can be seen in the table below.


\begin{table}[!ht]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|l|l|l|}
    \hline
        Maximum daily work hours & 8 & 9 & 10 & 11 & 12 \\ \hline
		Weekly skill score & infeasable & 2522.3 & 2530.4 &  2535.4 & 2539.4\\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

As you can see in the table above, increasing the maximum hours a technician can work a day does increase the total 
skill score for that week but only by at most 5. This may not be worth the increase if it increases costs of the project.
On the other hand if we decrease the daily hours we find that the score decreased by 7.1 for 9 hours. Also we can see
that 9 hours a day is the minimum amount of time required to complete all tasks described by the data as the 8 hour 
limit returned a infeasable model.

\subsection*{Communication 9}
With the additional constraint of technichans rosterd to do "Nightly telescope operation" required not to do
any tasks before hand on the same day, the score was once again reduced to 2489.3.
Similarly to the previous communication, this decrease is due to lossing some efficincy from other
jobs due to those who work the noght shift not being chosen for any day shifts.

\subsection*{Communication 10 - Final Model}
An assumption was made when incorperating the additional weekly training which was each
technichan can only receive one training session each. This means, of the five technicians 
receiving training they will all be unique and only increase in one skill only.
By giving additional training to five technicians and increasing their score in one skill by seven, 
this drastically improves the weekly score to 2979.3. The technicians you should train and the 
skills they should improve are indicated in the table below.

\begin{table}[!ht]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{|l|l|l|l|l|l|l|l|l|l|}
    \hline
        Technician & Skill \\ \hline
        5 & Environment monitoring\\ \hline
        6 & Mechanical maintenance\\ \hline
        7 & Environment monitoring\\ \hline
        10 & Environment monitoring\\ \hline
        12 & Software operation\\ \hline
    \end{tabular}
\end{table} 

However to give you an indication on whether a technician should be allowed to have multiple training sessions, the 
total weekly score would remain 2979.3 with technician 9 receving 3 sessions. As there is no change in the result
either model could be appropriate, but I will submit the model with the assumption stated an the begining of the
section as it aligns more with the spirit of Ancestral skies supplying training and education to the community 
as indicated in your breifing statement.\\

Although the roster does not need to change to maintain the weekly score of 2979.3, it could be improved 
if training at the beginning of each week continues and technicians scores keep increasing.
